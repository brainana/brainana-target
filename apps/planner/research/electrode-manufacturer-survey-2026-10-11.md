# Additional electrode manufacturers for BrainanaTarget

Reviewed 11 October 2026. This is an inclusion assessment based on manufacturer catalogs, relevance to chamber-based research and availability of physical specifications. It is not a market-share ranking. The manufacturers below are candidates; no new manufacturer presets have been added in this change.

## Recommended first additions

| Manufacturer | Families worth including | Relevance and configuration structure | Geometry/documentation needed before shipping |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **NeuroNexus** | Passive silicon designs; Vector Array for large-animal/deep recording | Highest priority with Cambridge. Select a catalog array design first, then packaging/connector. X-Series is a package/interface choice and should not become a different anatomical probe preset. | Exact shank outline, thickness, number/separation of shanks, site coordinates and shape, array/support junction, implantable length and mounting reserve. |

## Important consequences for the preset architecture

1. **Family → actual model/layout → measured/order-specific options.** Do not create a preset for every contact count. Do not offer the Cartesian product of all catalog dimensions: valid combinations belong to specific designs.
2. **Separate array, support and package.** A Vector Array manual describes a 15 mm recording array with additional support body and an 80 mm implantable assembly for those designs; newer technical descriptions discuss other build lengths. These are different configurations, not a universal family default. X-Series connector/cable choices should preserve anatomical geometry.
3. **Use a piecewise shaft envelope.** A silicon tip on a wider metal backbone needs more than one constant width/diameter. Target reach, bore fit and avoidance clearance must account for each segment that actually traverses the chamber or tissue.
4. **Store explicit contact shape and coordinates.** Tip contacts, planar pads, cylindrical macro rings, directional contacts and clustered tetrode contacts differ. Contact area alone does not identify pad shape; a tetrode's contact count does not imply shaft count.
5. **Support distinct device classes.** Penetrating probes, assembled arrays, concentric stimulators, optical/fluid assemblies and flexible cortical grids require different models. Acquisition systems and connector names remain metadata unless their bodies have defined collision geometry.
6. **Attach evidence to each field.** Record source URL, document/model revision, units, whether a value is published or user-measured, and whether its shape is schematic. Missing values should require input; no hidden invented dimensions should produce a supposedly feasible plan.
7. **Test transitions and physical constraints.** Changing model/count must update real sites, shaft envelopes, available options, reach and clearance diagnostics. Cancel/undo and legacy project migration must preserve physical configurations.

Recommended implementation order: NeuroNexus and Cambridge deep-brain families; Thomas and FHC single/tetrode/stimulation families; Alpha Omega research electrodes; ATLAS modular arrays. Optical assemblies and surface/clinical families follow when their geometry classes are supported. This ordering is an engineering assessment for the current research planner, not an assertion of relative sales or installed base.
